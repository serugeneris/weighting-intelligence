How we steered Qwen3.5-4B toward philosophy

Chapter 1

A neuron reads, then writes a fixed column

At every token, a layer receives the hidden state: 2,560 numbers that hold everything the model "knows" about this token so far. The layer's MLP (multi-layer perceptron) turns it into 9,216 neuron activations a₁…a₉₂₁₆, then down_proj maps them back to 2,560 numbers:

output = a₁·W_down[:,1] + … + a₉₂₁₆·W_down[:,9216]

So each neuron has two sides:

Read side: when it fires. Its activation aₙ depends on the input, so it changes every token. It can be negative, because Qwen's MLP computes silu(gate)·up and up can be negative.

Write side: what it writes. That is its column W_down[:, n], which never changes. The activation is only the volume knob.

Click Next token and the activations change but the columns don't. Hover a column to see one neuron alone. Tick Push neuron 3 to add that neuron's column on top, whatever it is doing at the time. That is what a neuron "push" is: the neuron doesn't fire harder, we inject its column directly.

Chapter 2

The residual stream is a running sum

Each token has its own stream of hidden states running up through the 32 layers. Every layer adds to it:

h(L+1) = h(L) + attention_L + MLP_L

A write at layer 8 can only affect what comes after it:

Later layers of the same token, which read the updated sum.

Later tokens, through attention, which copies information from earlier tokens forward.

It can't reach earlier layers, which have already run, or earlier tokens, because the model only looks backward.

Steering adds its vector at every token at layer 8, so every stream is nudged. Later layers read the nudge through non-linear steps, so they can amplify it, cancel it or ignore it. That is why the same kind of push gave on-task reflection at layer 8 but lyrical metaphysics at layer 16: it enters at a different depth and passes through a different amount of processing.

Chapter 3

The "philosophy direction" (diff-means)

We wrote 32 practical questions, each with a plain answer and a philosophical answer. For each answer we averaged the layer-8 hidden state over its tokens, giving one point per answer (2,560 numbers; 2 here).

The arrow from each plain answer to its philosophical twin says "this is what changed". The average of the 32 arrows is the diff-means direction: a single vector, not a matrix, that points from plain toward philosophical.

To steer, we add the same vector to every token's hidden state at that layer:

push = α × p50 × unit(direction)

unit(direction): the direction, rescaled to length 1. It says where to push.

p50: the median length of the hidden state at that layer. Hidden states grow as they go up the layers, so p50 gives each layer's natural scale.

α: our dial. At α = 0.5 the push is half the length of a typical hidden state.

Diff-means steering doesn't touch any neuron; it adds the vector directly. At layer 8 with α = 0.5, it was the best setting we found.

Chapter 4

Reading is not writing: why cosine

Our first idea was to push the neurons that fire most differently on philosophical text. They were very reliable detectors (t-statistic up to 22.8, consistent across all pairs), but pushing them produced loops like "being being being…".

The reason is that a push injects the neuron's column, and what matters is where that column points. We measure that with cosine similarity, the same measure used in embedding search:

cos(a, b) = (a · b) / (|a| · |b|)

It ignores length, which we set ourselves through the dose, and keeps only direction. Of a push of size 1, cos goes along the philosophy direction and √(1 − cos²) goes elsewhere.

In 2,560 dimensions almost everything is nearly perpendicular. Two random directions have a cosine of about 0 ± 0.02, so a cosine of 0.24 is far above chance, yet it still leaves about 97% of the push off target. That's why no single neuron worked.

Chapter 5

Rebuilding the direction from 16 neurons

No single column points the right way, but a few columns added together can. Matching pursuit (OMP) builds the target vector α·p50·direction step by step:

1. Pick the column most aligned with what is still missing.

2. Refit the weights (doses) of all columns picked so far with least squares, the choice that minimises the squared error. The columns overlap, so adding one changes the best weights for the others; refitting avoids double-counting.

3. Repeat k times.

The doses come from this math, not from trial and error. We then push all 16 neurons together and generate once.

Why ÷cos². Least squares gives the closest point the columns can reach, which falls short of the target. Its part along the direction is only cos² × α. Dividing the doses by cos² restores the full α along the direction, but the off-target part grows with it, which is part of why helpfulness drops.

Real numbers. At layer 8, 16 columns reached cos 0.45 (64 columns: 0.57; 256 columns: 0.73; source: runs/omp_rescaled.jsonl). Without rescaling, the group pushed only 0.45² ≈ 0.20 α along the direction, which is why our first groups did nothing. After ÷cos² the push along the direction is the full α, but the off-target part is about 2 α (√(1 − 0.45²) / 0.45 ≈ 1.98).

This toy has 20 dimensions, so a few columns can rebuild the target almost perfectly. In the model's 2,560 dimensions, 16 columns only reach cos 0.45.

Chapter 6

Weight edits: outer products, and no "+ constant"

The Astra paper (Sparks of Weight-Space Programming) edited W_down itself with a masked rank-1 matrix. vᵀ is the vector v laid on its side as a row. The outer product u·vᵀ multiplies a column by a row: cell (i, j) is uᵢ × vⱼ. Every column of the result is a scaled copy of u, so it has only one direction, which is what "rank 1" means. "Masked" means v is zero except on k chosen neurons.

Adding it to W_down gives neuron j an extra vⱼ·u in its column. At run time the output changes by:

Δoutput = (u·vᵀ)·a = u × (vᵀ·a)

The change always points along u, and its size is multiplied by the current activations a. On "how do I fix a drain?" the chosen neurons barely fire, so there is nothing to multiply.

Steering adds a constant, the same push every token, which is exactly what a bias term b in y = W·x + b would do. Qwen3.5's text MLPs have no bias at all. That is standard in large language models since PaLM (2022); Qwen's first technical report says it removed biases "following" PaLM. So a pure weight edit can rescale what neurons already write, but it can't add a behavior from nothing. Adding a 2,560-number bias to down_proj would turn our steering into a standalone checkpoint, and that is next step 1 in FINDINGS.md.

Glossary

Terms

Hidden state / residual stream
The 2,560 numbers per token that every layer reads and adds to; a running sum.
MLP neuron
One of 9,216 units per layer. Its activation aₙ is the read side; its column W_down[:, n] is the write side.
W_down / down_proj
The 2560×9216 matrix that turns neuron activations back into a change to the hidden state. It has no bias.
Diff-means direction
The average, over the 32 contrast pairs, of (philosophical answer's mean hidden state − plain answer's), at one layer. A single vector.
p50
The median length of the hidden state at a layer, measured on the plain answers. It sets the scale of a push.
α
Push size as a fraction of p50.
Dose
The size and sign of one neuron's push: we add dose × p50 × unit(column).
Cosine
Alignment between two vectors, ignoring length: 1 means the same direction, 0 means perpendicular.
t-statistic, consistency
Read-side statistics: how much more a neuron fires on philosophical answers than on plain ones, relative to the noise, and in what share of pairs.
OMP (orthogonal matching pursuit)
A greedy method that picks k columns and refits their weights with least squares to approximate a target vector.
÷cos² rescale
Corrects the OMP undershoot, so the push along the direction equals α.
Matched random control
Random neurons in the same layer, the same number of them, the same |doses| with random signs. It separates "these neurons" from "any push".
Outer product u·vᵀ
Column times row; a rank-1 matrix whose every column is a multiple of u.
Masked rank-1 edit
Adding u·vᵀ to W_down with v nonzero only on chosen neurons. Its effect is proportional to what those neurons are doing.
Bias
The constant b in y = W·x + b; a fixed push regardless of input. Qwen3.5's text MLPs have none.
Degenerate
The judge's flag for loops, gibberish or identity confusion.
