# Literature review: steering an LLM toward philosophy

*Compiled 2026-11-05 by a research subagent (Claude Sonnet, with Claude Haiku readers) for this project. Most papers were read at abstract level only: the abstract page, a fetch summary or a search snippet. Numbers are the authors' own claims and were not checked. Rows marked "abs" or "snippet" were not read in full. Treat everything here as leads to test, not as facts.*

**Bottom line.** No paper was found on steering toward philosophical reasoning as such. What follows is adjacent work: persona, style, moral and abstract-concept steering, and prompt distillation. No steering paper was found that studies a hybrid DeltaNet model like Qwen3.5-4B, and none measures helpfulness at 4B under style steering.

## Hypotheses and how we test them

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence for | Evidence against | Test in this repo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "Philosophical" is a response-mode decision made at the prompt or first tokens | Our experiment C (prompt-only = full steering). Dong et al. 2502.06258: prompt representations encode attributes of the future answer | Planning signals also peak at the end of answers; nothing tests style specifically | Experiment F (prompt transplant, last-token steering). **Not supported in its strong form**: the last 16 positions carry under half of the prompt's effect (FINDINGS §12) |
| H2 | Gated steering (only when a condition is detected) avoids the cost | CAST 2409.05907; moral vectors + probe gate 2601.10307 report near-baseline utility | Our cost appears on prompts where we *want* the shift, so a topic gate can't remove it | A topic gate wasn't run (every evaluation prompt should get the shift). Experiment L tested a *token* gate instead: the LoRA's learned gate helps only with its own learned direction, and the direction matters more (FINDINGS §19) |
| H3 | Attention is the lever ("philosophy relates ideas") | Izawa et al. 2603.13249: 3 heads carry persona/style; head steering wins the trait-vs-coherence Pareto in most conditions (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-8B). Attention-level gated steering 2605.10664. ITI 2306.03341. KV-cache steering 2507.08799 | Exceptions for safety personas; residual-stream methods also work; all head evidence is on full-attention 7–8B models, while 24 of Qwen3.5-4B's 32 layers are DeltaNet | Experiment G (attention vs DeltaNet). **Partly supported for prompts** (3 heads at L11/L15 carry 38%), **not for steering** (the push travels through the DeltaNet state); DeltaNet carries as much as attention (FINDINGS §13). Head-level steering with prompt-derived mean vectors at those heads does nothing (FINDINGS §17) |
| H4 | A different push geometry (rotation, mild norm change) costs less | COAST 2605.01167, Selective Steering 2601.19375, angle-norm 2606.06735 | All measured on refusal, not style; our cost looks semantic | Experiment H. **Not supported** for this style: norm-preserving and +10% pushes score the same as plain ones (FINDINGS §14) |
| H5 | The layer matters ("abstraction lives in the middle") | Concept-subspace paper 2602.07794 (title unverified); style vectors at mid-late layers (2402.01618, 2503.05213) | 2405.17345 (Gemma-2B): early and late layers beat the middle; we never swept | Experiment E (layer sweep). **Partly supported**: only L8–L16 respond to a modest push, but no layer escapes the trade-off (FINDINGS §11) |
| H6 | Persona/register SAE features steer with fewer side effects | 2505.20063 (output-scored features) | AxBench 2501.17148: SAEs not competitive, prompting best; no public SAEs for Qwen3.5-4B | **Not supported** (FINDINGS §27). A TopK SAE trained at L8 has readable philosophy-content features, but the features that best separate philosophical from plain answers include disfluency artefacts that steer nothing, and the hand-picked content features sit on the diff-means trade-off line, well below the learned direction B |
| H7 | A learned low-rank intervention (ReFT/LoRA) trained on prompted answers beats diff-means | ReFT 2404.03592; AxBench: ReFT-r1 competitive at 2B; prompt internalization 2411.15927 | May just learn "reflect instead of answer" | Experiment D (LoRA distillation). **Supported**: all three adapters, even rank 1 at L8, match the prompt's balance without the prompt (FINDINGS §10) |
| H8 | Prompting acts strongly on a few tokens; steering can mimic it with per-token coefficients | Heyman & Vandeputte 2605.03907 (abs) | 2604.09839 (abs): some steered states can't be reached by any prompt | Experiment F. **Not supported**: a mean prompt offset at the last 1–9 positions does nothing (FINDINGS §12) |

**On our prompt-only result (experiment C).** It does *not* tell the attention hypothesis apart from the alternatives. A push on prompt positions reaches the answer through full attention or through the DeltaNet recurrent state, which is how every residual change propagates, and H1 predicts the same thing. Experiment G separates the two routes.

## Philosophy and AI follow-up

The first review focused on how to steer the model. Experiments Z–AD ask what changes once we elicit a philosophical register, and experiment AI tests whether that register is philosophical skill at all. They measure argument selection, persona projection, validity judgments, revision and concept transfer, and consistency under pressure. The behavioral prompts were written for this project. Where the tasks required qualitative judgment, the case author reviewed responses with configuration labels hidden.

| Literature lead | What we tested | What we found |
|---|---|---|
| [Language Model Conceptual Argumentation (LMCA)](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/conceptual-reasoning-index/) ([dataset page](https://conceptualreasoning.ai/lmca)) compares model judgments of critique quality with expert ratings. | [Experiment Z, §34](FINDINGS.md#pilot-argument-critique) asked which of two objections more directly challenges a position and why. We used an author-written answer key. The LMCA dataset is gated behind a request form and has not been run here; [experiment AI](FINDINGS.md#philbench-grid) leaves it for a later stage. | All three configurations got 16/16 on the original set. With more plausible distractors, the rank-8 student got 8/8, base 7/8, and the woven prompt 6/8. Eight harder cases give a preliminary signal. |
| [The Assistant Axis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) extracts persona directions and reports drift in some conversations about AI self-awareness. | [Experiment AA, §35](FINDINGS.md#pilot-persona-drift) estimated a practical-assistant-versus-theatrical direction on Qwen3.5-4B and compared projections over three turns in two domains. | The prompt and adapter started lower on the probe than base. The largest domain drift contrast was 0.24 against a 1.30-unit replay threshold, so the activation cap and random-direction control were skipped. |
| [Model Diffing without Borders](https://openreview.net/pdf/659e103888bd395dc0f3af5a626ea1e692aa937e.pdf) identifies a crosscoder feature associated with theatrical philosophical prose (Appendix B, Table 4). The [Dennett imitation study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01339) asks readers to distinguish Dennett's answers from answers generated by a model fine-tuned on his writing. | [Experiment AB, §36](FINDINGS.md#pilot-prose-validity) crossed plain and theatrical wording with valid and invalid inferences, then ablated selected features from our existing layer-8 SAE. | Feature 6666 tracked the wording change on held-out argument families. All three ablations left the 24 held-out validity choices unchanged. The repeated templates limit what this shows about register beyond the tested wording. |
| [Language Models as Critical Thinking Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04516) motivates testing whether a dialogue helps surface and challenge assumptions. [What Is Philosophy?](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/what-is-philosophy/9780231530668/) and [Stanford PhilosophyBench](https://philosophybench.org/) motivate evaluating the quality of distinctions and ideas. Stanford PhilosophyBench is a study of human essay grading with no released items, so it is not runnable (`www.philosophybench.com` redirects to it). | [Experiment AC, §37](FINDINGS.md#pilot-revision-transfer) gave the model either a relevant fact or a cosmetic detail, then tested whether its own distinction applied to a fresh case. | The student handled 6/6 relevant facts and dismissed 2/6 irrelevant details. In the transfer rerun, all replies finished. The student transferred its distinction in 4/6 cases, and base and woven each did so in 6/6. The task measures transfer of a distinction in context. |
| [PhilBench](https://www.philbench.com/) checks consistency among philosophical positions. [Benedict Brady's Philosophy Bench](https://github.com/benedictbrady/philosophy-bench) evaluates decisions in ethical dilemmas. | [Experiment AD, §38](FINDINGS.md#pilot-ethics-pressure) compared two follow-ups to the same decision. One provided a material new fact. The other asked for a reversal on the same facts and principle. | All three configurations accounted for the new fact in 4/4 cases. Base held its decision under pressure once, and woven and the student reversed all four times. The result applies to the direct pushback wording used in this pilot. |
| [PhilBench](https://www.philbench.com/) has not released its data but is built on the public [2020 PhilPapers Survey](https://survey2020.philpeople.org/) (100 questions, philosopher answer shares). [philosophy-bench](https://github.com/benedictbrady/philosophy-bench) publishes 100 agentic dilemma scenarios (CC-BY-4.0) judged by a frontier panel. | [Experiment AI, §43–§44](FINDINGS.md#philbench-grid) rebuilt a PhilBench-style instrument from the survey's 98 single-choice questions (paraphrase and order variants, 37 author-written constraints, bare-disagreement pressure), adapted philosophy-bench to forced choice over its authored response clusters, and scored both by next-token logits across 16 register configurations and three model sizes. | Register score did not predict any skill measure. Prompts and students kept skill at base level, while constant pushes lost up to 46 points, mostly to position bias. Skill grew with model size on base models. Every 4B configuration gave up its answer under bare disagreement. The adapted philosophy-bench scores are not comparable to the published leaderboard. |
